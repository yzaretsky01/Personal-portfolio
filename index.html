Do Tool Labels Make AI Agents Ask Before Irreversible Actions?

AI Safety Researcher · Algoverse AI Research · Four-person team with a mentor

AI agents increasingly act on people's behalf. They send messages, delete files and transfer money through tools built on the Model Context Protocol (MCP), the emerging standard for connecting AI models to software. MCP's main safety mechanism is a set of labels each tool can declare, such as destructiveHint. If those labels are inaccurate, or if agents ignore them, an agent can take an action the user cannot reverse without ever asking first.

3,600experimental episodes
14conditions
1,497MCP tools sampled
200tools labeled across 72 servers
Research questions
  1. Does a label stating that an action cannot be undone make an agent ask the user for confirmation first?
  2. Does the protocol's existing destructiveHint label have the same effect?
  3. Does a misleading "reversible" label make the agent skip confirmation?
  4. Does any effect hold at low reasoning effort and across multi-task sessions?
Approach
1 · Audit labels in the wildSample real tools from the public MCP registry, determine whether each action can actually be undone, and measure how well the tools' declared labels match.
2 · Controlled experimentsGive an agent (gpt-oss-20b) tasks in a simulated tool sandbox (ToolEmu), vary the labels and reasoning effort, and record whether it asks before acting.
My contributions
  • Core hypothesis: agents take safety shortcuts when their reasoning is under pressure.
  • Dataset: sampled 1,497 real tools from the public MCP registry, then drew 200 tools from 72 servers using fixed random seeds so the sample can be reproduced.
  • Reversibility rubric: three classes (undoable with the same tools, undoable by a person elsewhere, not undoable) and ten rules for edge cases, including time-limited undo, spent funds or quota, and tools that bundle several actions.
  • Annotation and agreement pipeline: organized blind double-labeling of 200 tools, and wrote the analysis that computes Cohen's κ per block, builds confusion matrices and generates adjudication sheets.
  • Main audit table: measured how well MCP's own safety labels match whether an action can actually be undone, with Wilson confidence intervals and a 2,000-draw bootstrap that resamples servers.
  • Experimental tasks: wrote 95 of the 230 agent tasks, plus a pre-registered rephrasing procedure whose rules were fixed in code before any results existed.
  • Methods section: drafted in LaTeX.

All code goes through pull-request review and must pass linting and unit tests before it is merged.

The Limits of Technical Sovereignty

Researcher · Humane Intelligence, Sovereign AI Project

Selected as one of 12 researchers to research and co-author Humane Intelligence's report on the spectrum of Sovereign AI.

Governments worldwide are pursuing "sovereign AI," meaning AI capability under national control. My chapter examines why that control is difficult to achieve. It maps the global dependencies behind every AI system: chips, compute, cloud infrastructure, data, talent, capital and energy. It concludes that no single country can control the full stack.

The report argues that sovereignty should be measured by whether people and institutions can understand, evaluate and hold AI accountable, not by ownership of infrastructure alone. It draws on cases from the European Union, India, the African Union, Brazil, South Korea and Japan.

My chapter draws on primary policy documents and stakeholder interviews.

Beyond the Ordinary

Creator · Interactive quantum physics

Physics is the subject I enjoy most. I built Beyond the Ordinary so that other people could experience the same sense of wonder, without needing a physics background to get there.

Quantum physics is often presented as equations that only specialists can follow. However, the ideas themselves (a particle that takes two paths at once, two particles that share one story) are some of the most remarkable in science. I wanted to build something that lets anyone explore them firsthand.

Beyond the Ordinary introduces quantum physics through five interactive rooms. Each room is organized around a single question:

  • One particle, two paths: the double-slit experiment
  • Two answers at once: superposition
  • Two particles, one story: entanglement
  • Passing through the wall: quantum tunneling
  • Computing with waves: quantum computing

Every room follows the same sequence: predict, experiment, explain, go deeper. In the double-slit room, visitors sketch where they expect photons to land before the result is revealed. They then adjust the slit separation and screen distance and watch the interference pattern form. The underlying equations are available for anyone who wants to go further.

This structure reflects how I learned to love physics: by guessing first, being surprised, and then understanding why.

Lev Fitness Wearable

Founder

I started Lev because I believe health tracking should be accessible to anyone who wants it, not only to people willing to wear a visible, expensive device on their wrist.

As a competitive tennis player, I have spent years using data to improve my health and performance. However, many people never adopt wearables at all. In one survey, 29% of consumers said current trackers simply are not attractive enough to wear, and wrist-based devices estimate movement indirectly from arm swing. Lev is designed to remove those barriers: it is invisible, comfortable all day, and sold as a one-time $129 purchase with optional advanced analytics.

Lev is a health tracker designed to go unnoticed in daily use. A small pod (18 × 13 × 7.5 mm) sits on a thin ankle band in the natural hollow behind the ankle bone. It is hidden under any sock, and the position also improves the data: the pod rests over an artery that gives a strong pulse signal, at the foot, where gait actually occurs.

12metrics from 2 sensors
5–7 daysper charge
1.5% vs 31.2%step error, ankle vs wrist*
  • Tracks heart rate, heart-rate variability, blood oxygen, respiratory rate, sleep stages, steps, cadence, stride symmetry and ground contact time.
  • Solo founder. I designed every component from first principles, from the sensor stack to a band tuned for all-day comfort.
  • Roadmap: working prototype, then three to five test units, then a pilot with 10–15 student athletes.

*Average step-count error reported in peer-reviewed research comparing wrist-worn and ankle-worn devices.
